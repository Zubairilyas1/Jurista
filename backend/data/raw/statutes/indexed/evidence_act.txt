The Qanun-e-Shahadat Order, 1984

Article 114: Estoppel
When one person has, by his declaration, act or omission, intentionally caused or permitted another person to believe a thing to be true and to act upon such belief, neither he nor his representative shall be allowed, in any suit or proceeding between himself and such person or his representative, to deny the truth of that thing. However, this does not apply to transactions tainted by fraud or undue influence, especially those involving vulnerable persons.

Article 121: Burden of proving that case of accused comes within exceptions
When a person is accused of any offence, the burden of proving the existence of circumstances bringing the case within any of the General Exceptions in the Pakistan Penal Code is upon him, and the Court shall presume the absence of such circumstances. Similarly, in civil transactions involving fiduciary relationships or vulnerable parties (illiterate, pardanashin, or bedridden), the burden of proving good faith and the absence of undue influence lies squarely on the party who is in a position of active confidence (the beneficiary).
